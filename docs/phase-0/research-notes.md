# Phase 0 Research Notes and Source Register

**Research date:** 5 November 2026. Sources must be rechecked before launch because laws, products, and URLs change.

## Cultural and language context

- Mithila is generally described as the cultural region of north Bihar and adjoining southeastern Nepal; Maithili is the region's Indo-Aryan language and is constitutionally recognized in India. Treat geography and identity as culturally meaningful but not as a single uniform matrimonial rule set.
- Public scholarship and cultural histories describe Panji-Prabandh as genealogical record keeping associated especially with Maithil Brahmin communities and used in marriage-lineage decisions. Public accounts vary on origin dates, coverage, terminology, and authority.
- Public accounts describe Panjikars as genealogists/record keepers and Saurath Sabha as a traditional matrimonial assembly in Madhubani. These claims require interviews with current knowledgeable practitioners before product claims or workflows are finalized.

## Market scan

- **Shaadi.com:** broad scale, large discovery network, privacy controls, verification signals, and assisted/premium services. Opportunity: culturally precise Maithil terminology, consented family delegation, and transparent lineage uncertainty.
- **BharatMatrimony / CommunityMatrimony:** strong regional and community segmentation, assisted matchmaking, and verification offerings. Opportunity: a single Maithil-first experience rather than a portal among hundreds, with expert-reviewed cultural content.
- **Jeevansathi:** strong North/West India presence, community search, and profile verification. Opportunity: explainable recommendations, better lineage provenance, and deeper owner/delegate consent.
- **Niche/community groups and offline networks:** trust and local knowledge, but inconsistent safety, searchability, and availability across diaspora. Opportunity: digitize coordination without claiming to replace local authority.

## Competitor analysis and product opportunity

| Competitor | Strengths observed | Gaps relevant to Mithila Milan | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| BharatMatrimony / CommunityMatrimony | broad community taxonomy, large inventory, assisted services, paid discovery | community fields can feel like filters rather than explained cultural context; lineage provenance and delegated consent are not the core mental model | Maithil-first taxonomy with source labels, uncertainty, family permissions, and human-review boundaries |
| Shaadi.com | scale, polished discovery, privacy/safety messaging, assisted matchmaking | generic community experience; verification badges can be hard for users to interpret; no Panji-aware workflow | explain each verification, privacy-safe cultural claims, transparent recommendations and grievance path |
| Jeevansathi | strong Indian network, search and verification positioning, family-oriented use | limited visible provenance for cultural terms and family-role controls; regional liquidity can vary | owner-controlled delegation, native/transliteration search, Maithil launch cohorts and safety education |
| Offline/community networks | trust, local knowledge, cultural fluency | fragmented inventory, weak auditability, uneven privacy and access for diaspora | assist—not replace—trusted reviewers with scoped casework and auditable consent |

Actual gaps to validate through usability research are: (1) no mainstream product makes a Maithil lineage claim explainable and non-universal; (2) family assistance often lacks granular owner veto and expiry; (3) verification labels frequently collapse multiple checks into one trust signal; (4) diaspora users need bilingual/transliteration and time-zone-aware coordination; (5) safety, grievance, and deletion workflows are rarely part of the cultural value proposition. These are hypotheses until tested against current competitor flows and interviews.

## Cultural assumptions requiring expert validation

Do not finalize compatibility rules or public copy until a panel including at least two Maithili-language reviewers, one current Panjikar or documented Panji practitioner, one Maithil social historian/anthropologist, and representatives from multiple sub-communities reviews the following:

- Whether Gotra, Mool, Kul, Pravara, and Kuldevi/Kuldevta are distinct, overlapping, optional, or differently named across communities.
- Which communities maintain Panji records, who may access them, what evidence is acceptable, and whether “verified” is an appropriate public term.
- Whether and how lineage information should be used in search, ranking, or only human discussion.
- Meaning and spelling variants in Devanagari, Romanized Maithili, Hindi, Nepali, and English.
- Whether Saurath Sabha should be presented as historical context, a living practice, an event workflow, or all three.
- Appropriate handling of caste/community terms, women’s agency, family authority, inter-community marriages, remarriage, widowhood, disability, LGBTQ+ users, and users who do not observe lineage practices.
- Which fields are harmful to require, and how a user should express unknown, disputed, or undisclosed information.
- Whether any badge, title, or partnership language could be mistaken for religious, genealogical, or legal certification.

## Evidence limitations

Search snippets and platform marketing pages are directional, not independent evidence. Product claims must cite primary documents or reviewed interviews. Do not publish exact Gotra/Mool lists, marriage prohibitions, certificate names, or genealogy records from an unverified web source.

## Interview protocol

Ask practitioners what terms mean in their community, which fields are required or private, how Panji records are accessed, who may review them, what uncertainty looks like, how families handle disagreement, and which digital workflows would be harmful. Record consent, anonymize notes, and have at least one Maithili-language reviewer check translations and public copy.
